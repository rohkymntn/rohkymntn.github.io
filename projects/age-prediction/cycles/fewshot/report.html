Abstract

Epigenetic age predictors are sensitive to population composition, blood-cell source and assay design, yet target-domain labels are often scarce. We evaluated a neural representation trained by episodic optimization and adapted by differentiable ridge regression in two independent methylation cohorts. The source analysis comprised 542 training samples and 92 samples reserved for checkpoint selection; external evaluation comprised 588 query participants separated from support pools of 8, 16 or 32 labelled individuals. At 16 labels, the augmented model achieved mean absolute errors (MAEs) of 7.293 years in whole blood and 3.670 years in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), compared with 7.620 and 4.053 years for support-selected classical comparators. The corresponding paired MAE reductions were 0.326 years (95% conditional interval, 0.185–0.472; Holm-adjusted P = 0.0002) and 0.384 years (0.211–0.573; P = 0.0002). A frozen source ridge model retained lower error in whole blood, and affine recalibration was statistically indistinguishable from the neural adapter in PBMCs. These results identify a reproducible advantage over support-set model selection while delimiting the conditions under which few-shot adaptation improves upon fixed or assay-matched source models.

Results

Source-domain learning and independent external evaluation

Healthy adult control samples from GSE41037 and control women from GSE19711 provided 634 source samples. Discovery plate H (92 samples) was reserved for source-only checkpoint selection; the remaining 542 samples supplied feature selection, preprocessing, source-clock fitting and neural training. The model used 1,024 CpGs selected exclusively in the source-training partition. The external whole-blood cohort GSE40279 contained 656 participants aged 19–101 years. The PBMC release GSE37008 contained 99 deposited arrays; 92 participants aged 24–45 years remained after the specified quality filters and identifier audit.

Fixed random support pools contained 128 whole-blood participants and 32 PBMC participants, leaving 528 and 60 query participants. Query participants never entered model fitting, support-only baseline selection or checkpoint selection. Twenty seeded support draws were evaluated at each label budget, with the 16-shot comparison specified as primary. The target releases contained observations for 834 and 809 of the 1,024 selected probes; entirely unavailable probes were imputed using source-training medians.

Few-shot adaptation improves upon support-selected comparators

At the prespecified 16-label endpoint, the neural adapter reduced MAE by 4.3% in whole blood and 9.5% in PBMCs relative to the comparator selected by cross-validation within each support set. Both paired contrasts remained significant after Holm correction across cohorts. Inference is conditional on the recorded support draws and fixed query populations and therefore does not quantify uncertainty across independently sampled cohorts or subsequent model-development decisions.

The whole-blood advantage was largest at eight labels and decreased with additional target-domain supervision. In PBMCs, neural predictions remained close to affine recalibration across label budgets. The full 16-label comparison is reported below to distinguish the prespecified support-selected contrast from comparisons with individual estimators.

Performance relative to fixed classical estimators

In whole blood, frozen source ridge achieved an MAE of 6.842 years, compared with 7.293 years for the augmented neural model; linear and radial-basis-function residual kernels yielded MAEs of 7.038 and 7.052 years, respectively. In PBMCs, affine recalibration achieved an MAE of 3.854 years. The neural improvement relative to affine recalibration was 0.185 years, with a conditional interval that included zero. Thus, the primary result is specific to support-set model selection rather than uniform superiority over each classical estimator. The discrepancy further indicates that cross-validation among candidate adapters is unstable when only 16 target labels are available.

Augmentation and assay-availability diagnostics

Removal of synthetic feature augmentation produced MAEs of 7.266 years in whole blood and 3.656 years in PBMCs, marginally below the corresponding augmented estimates. No predictive benefit from the evaluated augmentation distribution was observed. Source-validation trajectories also varied across checkpoints, supporting checkpoint selection on data isolated from episodic training.

A post hoc assay-matching analysis refitted the source ridge model using only probes represented in each target dataset. Regularization was selected by grouped cross-validation in the source data, without access to target ages. The assay-matched model achieved MAEs of 6.094 years in whole blood and 7.893 years in PBMCs; offset-only calibration yielded 7.088 and 4.020 years, respectively. The opposing cohort-specific effects indicate that missing-probe structure and mean calibration shift are insufficient to account for the observed transport error. Because this analysis was specified after the primary evaluation, it is interpreted as a diagnostic rather than confirmatory evidence.

Discussion

Episodic neural adaptation reduced error relative to a prespecified support-selected comparator in two external cohorts, but its performance relative to fixed classical estimators depended on tissue and assay context. In particular, the unadapted source predictor remained strongest in whole blood, whereas neural adaptation was competitive with affine recalibration in PBMCs. These results separate adaptation performance from the reliability of model selection under limited supervision and argue for explicit reporting of both quantities.

Future models should estimate the expected benefit of adaptation conditional on assay coverage, tissue composition and source–target divergence. Any model developed using the present external results would require evaluation in an additional untouched cohort, because GSE40279 and GSE37008 would then constitute development data. The current partitions, predictions and statistical contrasts are therefore retained as a fixed record of this evaluation cycle.

Methods

Preprocessing and source prediction

GSE37008 reports normalized M-values, as documented in its sample-table definition. These were converted to beta values using β = 1/(1 + 2−M) before source scaling. An initial PBMC evaluation incorrectly used M-values directly and was invalidated during the final scale audit. The corrected evaluation retained every model weight, hyperparameter grid and support/query partition. The correction record and input-scale audit document this replacement. Only corrected PBMC results appear here.

Source-training medians imputed missing measurements. Age-correlation screening selected 1,024 eligible CpGs, followed by source-training standardization and clipping at ±8 standard deviations. Source ridge regularization was chosen from α ∈ {10, 100, 1000} by group-held-out source validation. Feature selection preceded this internal alpha comparison; its development scores are therefore not unbiased generalization estimates. The external query comparisons remain independent of that selection. Source-training neural episodes used group-out-of-fold clock predictions to construct residual targets. GSE19711 ages refer to recruitment rather than necessarily the exact blood-collection date.

Differentiable few-shot adaptation

The encoder comprised linear layers of dimensions 1,024 → 128 → 32 with LayerNorm, GELU and tanh. Its output was concatenated with a learned scaling of the source-clock prediction. For support embeddings H and source-clock residuals r, centered kernel ridge regression fitted a residual correction with a learned positive regularization parameter. The query prediction was the source-clock estimate plus the support-fitted residual intercept and kernel correction. Gradients through the linear solve trained the encoder, regularization and clock-coordinate scale.

Each of six T4 GPU runs used 2,000 source-domain episodes and AdamW, with three seeds per augmentation condition. Episode support and query samples were disjoint. Augmentation applied task-level Gaussian shifts, multiplicative scales and feature masking to encoder inputs while keeping the supplied source-clock values fixed. Twelve fixed 16-shot episodes from the source-validation plate selected checkpoints every 100 training steps. Predictions were ensembled over the three seeds within each condition.

Classical comparators and statistical analysis

The baseline family comprised unchanged source ridge, support median, affine recalibration, target-only ridge, linear residual kernel adaptation and RBF residual kernel adaptation. Kernel regularization used α ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}; four-fold cross-validation within each support set selected hyperparameters and the primary classical comparator. All methods used identical support/query partitions and source-selected input features. The fixed external query labels were accessed only for evaluation.

For each query participant, absolute errors were averaged over 20 support draws. Ten thousand paired participant bootstrap samples supplied conditional intervals; conditional two-sided sign-flip tests supplied P values. Sign-flip inference assumes exchangeability of signed paired losses. Holm correction covered the two primary cohort contrasts, with secondary contrasts corrected as a separate exploratory family. Shared support pools, source-model retraining uncertainty and uncertainty from selecting the fixed support pool are not captured by these intervals. At 32 shots in PBMCs, the complete 32-person pool is reused; only support-CV assignments vary. Two external cohorts do not establish general population-wide validity.
