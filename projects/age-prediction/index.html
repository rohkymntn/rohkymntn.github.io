Age prediction across blood methylation and skin microbiome

A public-data reconstruction of the 2023 W3PHIAI concept, with participant-level validation, an independent blood cohort, and a test of what the data can support about integration.

Kun Hyung Roh · analysis executed September 5, 2026

Blood methylation predicted age with a 4.21-year mean absolute error under plate-held-out validation. Skin microbiome error was 9.17 years with participants held out and 18.35 years with studies held out. The central finding is the gap between learning age-associated patterns inside a cohort and generalizing to a new population.

The original abstract was titled Integration of Blood Methylome and Skin Microbiome Data in Machine Learning Algorithms for Accurate Age Prediction (W3PHIAI, Washington, DC, 2023; bibliographic record). Its original code and data were unavailable. Everything below is a newly executed 2026 analysis, not a recovered 2023 result. The public cohorts used here do not contain verified blood/skin measurements from the same people, so this project does not report a validated combined model or a multimodal accuracy gain.

1. Start with the unit of evidence

I downloaded the released abundance matrix and metadata from Huang et al. and processed whole-blood methylation matrices from GEO. The methylation discovery cohort was GSE41037: 720 deposited samples, filtered to 360 healthy controls with the source's QC flag set to “yes” and age at least 18. The external cohort was GSE19711: 274 control women, with ages recorded at recruitment. Both matrices contained the same 27,578 CpG identifiers.

The skin matrix contained 1,975 samples and 7,311 ASVs, but only 340 study-qualified subject identifiers. In Qiita study 11052, all 177 rows had the same subject identifier while the age values included both 31 and 36. I excluded that identity-ambiguous study before model fitting, leaving 1,798 samples from 339 participant identifiers across three studies. Thirteen remaining participant identifiers had a one-year age difference across repeated samples. Those samples retained their recorded ages and stayed together in every participant-held-out split.

This is not a cosmetic distinction. Study 1841 supplies 1,293 samples from 83 participant identifiers, whereas study 10317 supplies 440 from 237. Counting each row equally would give the repeatedly sampled, predominantly younger cohort disproportionate influence. The skin models therefore receive inverse-sample-count training weights, and reported primary errors assign equal total weight to each participant.

2. Fit the models inside the validation loop

Blood methylation. Five outer folds held out entire assay plates; the eligible discovery samples occupied seven plates. Within each outer training set, three-fold plate-grouped validation selected 100 or 500 CpGs and ridge regularization α ∈ {10, 100, 1000}. Training-median imputation, age-correlation feature selection and standardization were refitted inside every inner fold. After internal evaluation, a final configuration was selected using only the discovery cohort and applied once to the external controls. No external ages were used for feature selection, fitting or calibration.

Skin microbiome. A sample-local Hellinger transformation (square root of relative abundance) followed training-only prevalence filtering at 1%. A 160-tree random forest used square-root feature subsampling; minimum leaf sizes of 1, 3 or 5 were selected inside three-fold participant-grouped validation. Five outer folds held out all samples from each test participant. The source table had already undergone age-independent prevalence filtering; this upstream, cohort-wide preprocessing could not be undone from the released processed matrix.

Controls. Training-age median baselines accompany both modalities. A study/site/sex-only ridge model tests how much age can be inferred from non-microbial metadata. A fixed forest with minimum leaf size 3 is evaluated under random-sample, participant-held-out and leave-one-study-out splits to isolate the consequences of changing the validation unit. Ten shuffled-label runs per modality refit preprocessing and models; skin labels are permuted at the participant level. A separate unshuffled run uses exactly the same model and target definition as each permutation control.

Blood MAE weights samples equally; skin MAE weights participants equally. Intervals use 2,000 resamples of plates for internal blood evaluation, samples for external blood evaluation, and participants for skin. They are conditional on the fitted predictions and do not include uncertainty from retraining. These are different cohorts and age distributions, so the table is not a controlled ranking of blood versus skin as biological modalities.

The blood model explains much of the age variation in plate-held-out discovery data (R² = 0.91). In external control women aged 52–78, MAE increases to 6.03 years and R² is -1.38. A negative external R² means that, under squared error, these predictions are worse than the constant external-cohort mean. The model still beats its training-median age baseline, but that baseline is weak under such a large age-distribution shift. Reporting only the favorable MAE comparison would hide this failure.

For skin, the metadata-only model gives 10.06-year MAE, versus 9.17 for the microbial model. The participant-bootstrap reduction is 0.89 years (95% conditional interval 0.46–1.32). Microbial features add information in these held-out participants, but study and demographic structure already explain a substantial portion of the predictive signal. The model increasingly underpredicts age in older participants.

4. Interrogate the failures

Repeated participants and study transfer

With random sample splitting, 90.5% of test samples have a participant represented in training. Both participant and study holdouts reduce that overlap to zero. For the fixed forest, participant-weighted MAE changes from 8.93 years under random sampling to 9.24 under participant holdout: an increase of 0.31 years, with a conditional participant-bootstrap interval of 0.15–0.48. The observed numerical difference is modest; the identity overlap is nevertheless a material difference in what the benchmark measures.

The larger effect is the evaluation population. For the same participant-held-out predictions, sample-weighted error is 6.44 years while participant-weighted error is 9.24 years. Leaving an entire study out raises the latter to 18.35 years. Study 1841 is largely young adults, and study 10317 covers a much broader, older population. The result is consistent with substantial cohort and age-distribution dependence, rather than a portable skin-aging clock. This is a new benchmark with different inclusion rules, transformations, weights and model settings; it is not a direct reproduction of the published study's headline accuracy.

Missing methylation measurements and implausible predictions

Two external controls have more than 1% missing CpGs. One has approximately 57% missing measurements and an unconstrained ridge prediction of −28.4 years at recorded age 74. The other large negative outlier is also highly incomplete. Predictions were neither clipped to plausible ages nor silently removed.

A post hoc quality sensitivity analysis applies a 1% missingness threshold to the locked external predictions: 272 samples remain, MAE becomes 5.48 years and R² remains -0.26. That check identifies an actionable assay-quality problem, but it does not rescue independent generalization and is not a replacement for the all-sample primary result. In deployment, a quality gate and an abstention policy would need to be specified and validated before use.

Feature interpretation and negative controls

The fitted blood ridge model assigns large standardized coefficients to CpGs annotated to NHLRC1, SCGN and ASPA; all three were selected in all five outer training sets. Coefficients are conditional on correlated features and the training scale, not causal effects of those genes. The full CpG list, coefficients and selection frequencies are downloadable. Skin impurity ranks include ASVs assigned to Mycoplasma, Enterobacteriaceae and other taxa. Those ranks are training-set summaries, can reflect cohort structure, and do not establish microbial drivers of aging. Taxonomic assignments come from the released annotation and are provisional, especially at species level.

Matched unshuffled models outperform the ten shuffled-label controls in each modality. This supports the presence of learnable age-associated structure in the chosen datasets. Ten permutations are a coarse diagnostic; they do not establish a high-resolution significance level or validate a mechanism.

5. Why these data do not validate integration

A genuine combined clock needs blood and skin measurements from the same participants, with outcomes held out at the participant level. Pairing unrelated people because their ages match would use the prediction target to manufacture the joint data. It could make an attractive scatterplot while answering a different, invalid question.

For a weighted average of two unbiased errors, its variance is

Var(w eblood + (1 − w)eskin) = w²σ²blood + (1 − w)²σ²skin + 2w(1 − w)Cov(eblood, eskin).

Separate cohorts do not identify that within-person covariance. Moreover, the observed marginal errors here come from different populations. They cannot be substituted into this expression to claim a measured fusion gain. I included a tested pairing utility that rejects disjoint participant IDs, duplicate participant rows and outcome columns among features. It is a guardrail for future paired data, not a trained or evaluated multimodal model.

6. Reproduce and extend

The archive includes the executed analysis, derived predictions, metrics, data audit, annotation tables and all three figures as PNG, SVG and PDF. Source URLs are pinned to a Git commit for the microbial data, and every downloaded file has a SHA-256 checksum. Raw third-party matrices are retrieved by the download script rather than republished in the archive.

python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python src/download.py
python src/prepare.py
python src/analyze.py
python src/diagnostics.py
python src/figures.py
python src/test_analysis.py

The scientific integrity checks cover group separation, exclusion of the identity-ambiguous cohort, metric recomputation, external-control eligibility, and rejection of invalid cross-person fusion. A future extension should obtain a consented, paired cohort with synchronized sampling and verified participant IDs; predefine quality gates and holdouts; and compare each modality with a combined model on the same untouched participants.

Scope. These models estimate recorded chronological age. They have not been validated as biological-age, health-risk or clinical decision tools. Processed public data limit control over upstream normalization; participant identity is only as reliable as the deposited metadata; external blood age is recorded at recruitment; and three skin studies are too few to establish broad transportability. Figure quality does not change those evidential limits.
