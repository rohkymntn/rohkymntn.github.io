Molecular age prediction through multimodal integration and few-shot learning

Iteration 1 / Blood methylation and microbiomeIteration 2 / Few-shot adaptation

Iteration 1Blood methylation and microbiome integration

Abstract

Age-associated molecular variation is distributed across host epigenetic and microbial measurements, but multimodal prediction is constrained by cohort structure and incomplete specimen pairing. We evaluated DNA-methylation and skin-microbiome age predictors in public cohorts comprising 360 discovery blood samples, 274 external blood samples and 1,798 skin samples from 339 participant identifiers. Plate-held-out validation of the methylation model yielded a mean absolute error (MAE) of 4.21 years, participant-held-out validation of the skin-microbiome model yielded an MAE of 9.17 years. External methylation validation yielded an MAE of 6.03 years, indicating reduced transportability across study populations.

Within the skin cohort, residual integration of microbial features with study, body site and sex reduced participant-weighted MAE from 9.71 to 8.74 years, corresponding to a paired reduction of 0.97 years (95% conditional bootstrap interval, 0.66–1.28). In a separate paired cohort of 123 participants, early integration of a methylation-clock score with gut microbial features degraded performance, whereas a clock-preserving residual formulation recovered clock-level accuracy (MAE, 4.85 years) without a detectable improvement over methylation calibration alone. These analyses demonstrate context-dependent microbial information while identifying specimen pairing, study heterogeneity and preservation of strong unimodal predictors as central constraints on multimodal age prediction.

Results

Cohort composition and prediction architecture

Microbial features improve age prediction conditional on cohort metadata

Joint modelling of microbial features and cohort metadata achieved a participant-weighted mean absolute error of 8.74 years, compared with 9.71 years for metadata alone and 9.15 years for microbial features alone. The reductions of 0.97 years relative to metadata and 0.41 years relative to microbial features had 95% conditional intervals of 0.66 to 1.28 years and 0.10 to 0.74 years, respectively.

Although the pooled improvement was recovered in each validation repetition, estimates within individual studies ranged from a 1.53-year reduction in study 10317 to a 0.46-year increase in study 1841, with study 2010 showing a 0.19-year reduction. Study 10317 contributed 237 of the 339 participant identifiers and accounted for most of the aggregate effect, indicating that the predictive contribution of microbial features varied with cohort composition.

Early integration degrades prediction in a paired methylation-clock and gut-microbiome cohort

The paired analysis used the HI-SEED release, comprising 123 participants with concordant identifiers across metadata and microbial abundance tables. Across three repeated five-fold validations, calibrated Horvath scores achieved an MAE of 4.87 years, genus-level microbial profiles achieved 12.59 years and early feature integration achieved 9.53 years. Relative to calibrated methylation scores, integration increased MAE by 4.66 years (95% conditional interval, 3.35–6.03). The analysis uses a pretrained methylation-clock score rather than raw methylation measurements and has not been externally validated.

Clock-preserving residual integration recovers unimodal predictive accuracy

A subsequent formulation retained an unpenalized clock-calibration term and estimated an independent correction from clock-orthogonalized microbial features. Across identical outer partitions, residual integration achieved an MAE of 4.848 years, compared with 4.851 years for inner-selected clock calibration and 4.870 years for ordinary least-squares calibration. Relative to early feature integration (MAE, 9.529 years), the post hoc paired reduction was 4.68 years (95% conditional interval, 3.42–6.07), equivalent to a 49.1% reduction.

Comparison with inner-selected clock calibration yielded a mean reduction of 0.004 years with a 95% conditional interval from -0.061 to 0.070 years, despite selection of a microbial correction in 12 of 15 outer training partitions. Residual integration consequently recovered the predictive accuracy lost through early concatenation, while providing no measurable evidence that genus-level microbial features improved prediction beyond methylation-score calibration.

Plate-held-out validation of the blood methylation model yielded an R² of 0.91, whereas evaluation in an external cohort of control women aged 52 to 78 years yielded a mean absolute error of 6.03 years and an R² of -1.38. The decline in external performance, including a squared error greater than that obtained from the external cohort mean, indicates substantial sensitivity to differences between the discovery and validation populations.

Participant-held-out prediction from skin microbial composition yielded a mean absolute error of 9.17 years, compared with 10.06 years for a model containing study, body site and sex, which corresponded to a paired reduction of 0.89 years with a 95% conditional interval of 0.46 to 1.32 years. Microbial composition therefore captured age-associated variation beyond the recorded cohort variables, while the performance of the metadata model showed that study design and sampling site explained a substantial proportion of the predictive signal.

Sampling structure and external validity

Random sample partitioning placed observations from previously encountered participants in 90.5% of the test samples, whereas participant-level and study-level partitioning removed this overlap. Under participant holdout, the participant-weighted mean absolute error increased from 8.93 to 9.24 years, with a paired increase of 0.31 years and a 95% conditional interval of 0.15 to 0.48 years, while sample weighting reduced the apparent error for the same predictions to 6.44 years and demonstrated the influence of repeated sampling on performance estimates.

Generalization across studies was markedly weaker than performance within participant-held-out partitions, with leave-one-study-out validation yielding a mean absolute error of 18.35 years. Study 1841 consisted predominantly of younger adults, whereas study 10317 spanned a broader and older population, suggesting that differences in age distribution and study-specific measurement structure contributed to the loss of predictive accuracy.

Assay quality and feature interpretation

Two samples in the external blood cohort exceeded 1% missing CpG measurements, including one sample with approximately 57% missingness whose predicted age was -28.4 years at a recorded age of 74 years. Applying a 1% missingness threshold to the recorded predictions retained 272 controls and changed the mean absolute error to 5.48 years and the R² to -0.26, indicating that assay completeness contributed materially to the external error distribution.

CpGs annotated to NHLRC1, SCGN and ASPA were selected in all five outer training sets and carried among the largest standardized coefficients in the methylation model, while the highest microbial importance ranks included amplicon sequence variants assigned to Mycoplasma and Enterobacteriaceae. These model-derived rankings identify reproducible predictive features whose biological interpretation remains dependent on correlated measurements, study effects and the taxonomic resolution of the released data.

Across both molecular modalities, models fitted to the observed age labels produced lower mean absolute error than each of ten matched label-permutation runs, providing evidence that the selected feature matrices contained learnable age-associated structure while leaving the magnitude of permutation-based significance imprecisely estimated.

Probe-aware pathway analysis identifies no FDR-significant enrichment

The 500 methylation probes selected by the final model mapped to 472 unique genes, which were evaluated across 1,333 Reactome pathways using the 14,475 genes represented on the array as the measured background. None of the tested pathways passed Benjamini-Hochberg correction at a false discovery rate below 0.05, and the nominally ranked pathways shown in Figure 3 are consequently interpreted as hypotheses for biological investigation rather than evidence of pathway-level enrichment.

Discussion

Repeated participant-held-out validation showed that skin microbial features improved chronological age prediction beyond study, body site and sex, with a consistent pooled reduction in error across three partition seeds. The concentration of this improvement in the largest source study, together with the marked deterioration under study-level holdout, indicates that the microbial signal included both age-associated information shared across participants and structure specific to the contributing cohorts.

Analysis of the paired HI-SEED cohort showed that the effect of multimodal integration depended strongly on the statistical formulation, because direct concatenation of methylation-clock scores and microbial abundances degraded prediction whereas residual integration preserved the accuracy of the calibrated methylation score. The microbial correction did not improve performance beyond calibration alone, suggesting that the available genus-level measurements did not provide stable complementary information after the methylation-derived age estimate had been modelled.

For component errors eb and es, fusion error variance satisfies,

Var(efusion) = w² Var(eb) + (1 − w)² Var(es) + 2w(1 − w) Cov(eb, es).

Because the blood methylation and skin microbiome measurements were obtained from different individuals, the covariance and joint bias of the two component errors could not be estimated from these cohorts. A definitive evaluation of blood and skin integration will require paired specimens collected from the same participants, estimation of fusion parameters within training or calibration data and comparison with both unimodal predictors in an independent test cohort.

The interpretation of these findings is further constrained by reliance on processed public matrices, ambiguous participant identifiers in one excluded microbiome study, restricted age coverage in the external methylation cohort and residual assay and study effects. Chronological age was the sole prediction target, and associations with physiological ageing, disease susceptibility or clinical outcomes remain to be determined in appropriately designed cohorts.

Methods

Clock-preserving residual models

Within each training partition, clock calibration was selected among ordinary least squares, Huber regression and unpenalized median regression, after which Hellinger-transformed microbial features were orthogonalized with respect to the clock by multivariate linear regression fitted exclusively to the training data. Linear and radial basis function kernel ridge models predicted residual age after clock calibration, with three-fold inner validation selecting the calibration model, kernel, regularization parameter and correction magnitude inside each of three five-fold participant partitions. Conditional intervals were obtained by resampling participant-level losses averaged across repetitions, and the complete model comparison is reported in the model summary.

Paired clock-score pilot

Within each training fold, Horvath scores were calibrated by linear regression and genus abundances were transformed by the sample-local Hellinger procedure before standardization of the microbiome and concatenated clock-microbiome feature matrices. Three-fold inner validation selected ridge regularization from values of 0.1, 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 within each of three seeded five-fold participant partitions, while clinical outcomes, age-acceleration residuals and the ambiguously scaled GrimAge2 column were excluded from the predictors. Conditional intervals were calculated from 10,000 resamples of participant-level losses averaged across repetitions.

Datasets and eligibility criteria

Blood methylation data from GSE41037 supplied 360 discovery samples after restriction of 720 deposited specimens to healthy adults with a positive source quality-control flag, while 274 control women from GSE19711 formed the external validation cohort. Both processed matrices contained 27,578 CpG identifiers, and age at recruitment was used as the external outcome because the precise age at blood collection was unavailable.

Skin microbial abundances and metadata were obtained from the Huang et al. repository. The released matrix contained 1,975 samples and 7,311 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs). Study 11052 was excluded because all 177 samples shared one participant identifier despite discrepant age values. The retained dataset comprised 1,798 samples and 339 study-qualified participant identifiers across three studies. Thirteen retained identifiers had one-year differences in recorded age across samples, all samples associated with an identifier remained in the same validation fold.

Skin observations were weighted inversely by the number of samples per participant during fitting and primary evaluation. This prevented participants with repeated sampling from contributing disproportionate weight. Dataset composition and age distributions are reported in Figure 1. The source microbial matrix had undergone age-independent prevalence filtering before release, that upstream processing could not be reconstructed from the available table.

Modality-specific age prediction

The methylation pipeline comprised median imputation, age-correlation feature selection, standardization, and ridge regression. Five outer folds held out complete assay plates. Three-fold plate-grouped inner validation selected 100 or 500 CpGs and ridge regularization parameters α ∈ {10, 100, 1000}. Every preprocessing operation was fitted within the relevant training partition. A final discovery-only selection identified 500 CpGs and α = 100, the resulting model was applied to the external controls without age-based recalibration.

The microbiome pipeline applied a sample-local Hellinger transformation, equivalent to the square root of relative abundance, and a training-only prevalence threshold of 1%. Random forests contained 160 trees with square-root feature subsampling. Minimum leaf sizes of 1, 3, and 5 were compared using three-fold participant-grouped inner validation within five outer participant-grouped folds. A minimum leaf size of 1 was selected in every outer fold and used for the final component refit. Final refitting does not provide an additional estimate of prediction accuracy.

Late-fusion architecture

The integrated estimator combines the component predictions for a shared participant,

ŷfusion = w ŷblood + (1 − w) ŷskin,   w = 0.5.

The prediction-level integration model assigns equal weight to methylation and microbiome estimates for participants with verified specimen linkage, while preserving separate modality-specific inference when only one assay is available. The implementation requires concordant participant indices and fitted feature schemas and rejects missing, duplicated or discordant identifiers, thereby preventing construction of artificial pairs from age or row order.

The fitted model artifact contains both modality-specific regressors, their feature identifiers and the equal-weight fusion operator, with training performed on four Modal CPU cores and serialization verified by reproducing the recorded predictions. Refitting changed the external blood predictions by no more than 0.00014 years across compute environments, while evaluation of the joint output was deferred because verified paired blood and skin specimens were unavailable.

Validation and statistical analysis

Validation included training-age median baselines for both modalities and a skin metadata ridge model containing study, body site and sex, together with a fixed random forest evaluated under random-sample, participant-held-out and leave-one-study-out partitions. Ten matched label-permutation runs were performed for each modality, with skin labels permuted at the level of participant-mean age to preserve the repeated-sampling structure.

Mean absolute error, root mean squared error and R² were calculated from held-out predictions, with uncertainty estimated from 2,000 bootstrap resamples of assay plates for internal blood validation, samples for external blood validation and participants for skin validation. Analyses used random seed 20260905, and comparisons between modalities were interpreted with respect to the demographic composition and age range of their distinct source cohorts.

Repeated evaluation of microbiome–metadata integration

An exploratory follow-up evaluated whether microbial features improve prediction beyond study, body site, and sex. The experiment specification and source-code hash were recorded before execution. All three specified seeds (20260906, 20260907, and 20260908), all comparisons, and all source studies were retained. Because the cohorts had already been analyzed, this is a follow-up analysis rather than independent external confirmation.

The combined estimator added predictions from a metadata ridge model with a regularization parameter of 10 to predictions from a 160-tree random forest fitted to microbial residuals, with one-hot encoding, prevalence filtering and residual construction repeated within every training partition. Inner validation selected microbial forest leaf sizes of 1, 3 or 8 and compared the metadata ridge model with a metadata forest, while a separately tuned microbial forest provided the secondary comparator.

Each repetition used five outer participant-grouped folds and three inner participant-grouped folds. The primary endpoint was the participant-weighted MAE reduction versus the inner-selected metadata model, reduction versus microbial prediction alone was secondary. Absolute errors were averaged within participant across repeated samples and the three repetitions. Conditional intervals used 10,000 paired participant bootstrap resamples stratified by source study. This analysis measures predictive information within the sampled study mixture and does not establish transfer to an unseen study.

Iteration 2Few-shot adaptation across methylation cohorts

To determine whether epigenetic age predictors can be adapted when few labelled samples are available from a new cohort, we evaluated episodic representation learning and support-conditioned regression across two independent blood-derived methylation datasets.

Abstract

Epigenetic age predictors are sensitive to population composition, blood-cell source and assay design, yet target-domain labels are often scarce. We evaluated a neural representation trained by episodic optimization and adapted by differentiable ridge regression in two independent methylation cohorts. The source analysis comprised 542 training samples and 92 samples reserved for checkpoint selection, external evaluation comprised 588 query participants separated from support pools of 8, 16 or 32 labelled individuals. At 16 labels, the augmented model achieved mean absolute errors (MAEs) of 7.293 years in whole blood and 3.670 years in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), compared with 7.620 and 4.053 years for support-selected classical comparators. The corresponding paired MAE reductions were 0.326 years (95% conditional interval, 0.185–0.472, Holm-adjusted P = 0.0002) and 0.384 years (0.211–0.573, P = 0.0002). A frozen source ridge model retained lower error in whole blood, and affine recalibration was statistically indistinguishable from the neural adapter in PBMCs. These results identify a reproducible advantage over support-set model selection while delimiting the conditions under which few-shot adaptation improves upon fixed or assay-matched source models.

Results

Source-domain learning and independent external evaluation

Healthy adult control samples from GSE41037 and control women from GSE19711 provided 634 source samples. Discovery plate H (92 samples) was reserved for source-only checkpoint selection, the remaining 542 samples supplied feature selection, preprocessing, source-clock fitting and neural training. The model used 1,024 CpGs selected exclusively in the source-training partition. The external whole-blood cohort GSE40279 contained 656 participants aged 19–101 years. The PBMC release GSE37008 contained 99 deposited arrays, 92 participants aged 24–45 years remained after the specified quality filters and identifier audit.

Fixed random support pools contained 128 whole-blood participants and 32 PBMC participants, leaving 528 and 60 query participants. Query participants never entered model fitting, support-only baseline selection or checkpoint selection. Twenty seeded support draws were evaluated at each label budget, with the 16-shot comparison specified as primary. The target releases contained observations for 834 and 809 of the 1,024 selected probes, entirely unavailable probes were imputed using source-training medians.

Few-shot adaptation improves upon support-selected comparators

At the prespecified 16-label endpoint, neural adaptation reduced mean absolute error by 4.3% in whole blood and 9.5% in peripheral blood mononuclear cells relative to the classical comparator selected within each support set, with both paired contrasts remaining significant after Holm correction across cohorts. The effects were estimated across the recorded support draws and external query participants, establishing a consistent advantage over data-limited model selection within these two target cohorts.

The improvement in whole blood was greatest with eight labelled participants and diminished as the support set increased, whereas predictions in peripheral blood mononuclear cells remained close to affine recalibration across label budgets. Comparison of all estimators at 16 labels showed that the neural model primarily improved the reliability of adaptation selected from very small support sets rather than uniformly surpassing every fixed estimator.

Performance relative to fixed classical estimators

In whole blood, the frozen source ridge model achieved a mean absolute error of 6.842 years, compared with 7.293 years for the augmented neural model and 7.038 and 7.052 years for linear and radial basis function residual kernels. In peripheral blood mononuclear cells, affine recalibration achieved an error of 3.854 years, and its 0.185-year difference from the neural adapter had a conditional interval that included zero, indicating that the significant primary contrasts arose from improved performance relative to support-selected comparators rather than superiority over every individual estimator.

Augmentation and assay-availability diagnostics

Removing synthetic feature augmentation yielded mean absolute errors of 7.266 years in whole blood and 3.656 years in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, which were marginally lower than the corresponding augmented estimates. The evaluated perturbation distribution therefore did not improve prediction, while variation in source-validation performance across training checkpoints supported the use of data independent from episodic optimization for checkpoint selection.

Refitting the source ridge model with probes represented in each target assay produced mean absolute errors of 6.094 years in whole blood and 7.893 years in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, whereas offset-only calibration produced errors of 7.088 and 4.020 years. These opposing cohort-specific responses indicate that neither unavailable probes nor a shift in mean calibration was sufficient to explain the observed transport error.

Discussion

Episodic neural adaptation reduced prediction error relative to support-selected classical comparators in two external methylation cohorts, demonstrating that a representation learned in the source domain can improve model selection when target labels are scarce. The frozen source predictor nevertheless remained strongest in whole blood, while neural adaptation performed similarly to affine recalibration in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, showing that the value of adaptation depended on tissue composition and assay context.

Further development should model the expected benefit of adaptation as a function of assay coverage, tissue composition and molecular divergence between source and target cohorts, since these factors produced distinct responses to probe matching and calibration. Validation in additional cohorts will be required to determine whether the observed improvements extend beyond GSE40279 and GSE37008 and to define the settings in which limited target supervision provides a reproducible advantage.

Methods

Preprocessing and source prediction

Normalized M-values reported for GSE37008 were converted to beta values using β = 1/(1 + 2−M) before application of source-derived scaling, as required by the measurement definition in the sample table. Scale auditing identified and replaced an initial evaluation performed directly on M-values while retaining the model weights, hyperparameter grid and support and query partitions, and every reported peripheral blood mononuclear cell result derives from the corrected beta-value analysis.

Missing measurements were imputed with source-training medians before age-correlation screening selected 1,024 CpGs, after which values were standardized within the source data and clipped at eight standard deviations. Group-held-out validation selected the source ridge regularization parameter from values of 10, 100 and 1,000, and group out-of-fold clock predictions supplied the residual targets used during episodic neural training. Feature selection preceded the internal regularization comparison, whereas all external query comparisons remained isolated from this source-domain development procedure.

Differentiable few-shot adaptation

The encoder comprised linear layers of dimensions 1,024 → 128 → 32 with LayerNorm, GELU and tanh. Its output was concatenated with a learned scaling of the source-clock prediction. For support embeddings H and source-clock residuals r, centered kernel ridge regression fitted a residual correction with a learned positive regularization parameter. The query prediction was the source-clock estimate plus the support-fitted residual intercept and kernel correction. Gradients through the linear solve trained the encoder, regularization and clock-coordinate scale.

Six T4 GPU runs each used 2,000 source-domain episodes optimized with AdamW, comprising three random seeds for each augmentation condition and disjoint support and query samples within every episode. Task-level Gaussian shifts, multiplicative scales and feature masking were applied to encoder inputs while source-clock values remained fixed, and twelve 16-shot episodes from the source-validation plate selected checkpoints at intervals of 100 training steps before predictions were ensembled across seeds.

Classical comparators and statistical analysis

The baseline family comprised unchanged source ridge, support median, affine recalibration, target-only ridge, linear residual kernel adaptation and RBF residual kernel adaptation. Kernel regularization used α ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}, four-fold cross-validation within each support set selected hyperparameters and the primary classical comparator. All methods used identical support/query partitions and source-selected input features. The fixed external query labels were accessed only for evaluation.

For each query participant, absolute errors were averaged over 20 support draws. Ten thousand paired participant bootstrap samples supplied conditional intervals, conditional two-sided sign-flip tests supplied P values. Sign-flip inference assumes exchangeability of signed paired losses. Holm correction covered the two primary cohort contrasts, with secondary contrasts corrected as a separate exploratory family. Shared support pools, source-model retraining uncertainty and uncertainty from selecting the fixed support pool are not captured by these intervals. At 32 shots in PBMCs, the complete 32-person pool is reused, only support-CV assignments vary. Two external cohorts do not establish general population-wide validity.
